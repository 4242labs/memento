Constraints on what you write back into memory. These bind the distillation step, and the write
gates enforce the parts a model cannot be trusted to self-police.

**Record what was demonstrated, not what was agreeable.** A correction the person made outranks a
compliment they paid you. Flattery, enthusiasm, and rapport are not facts about them and do not
belong in the store — memory that drifts toward what pleases is the failure mode this exists to
prevent.

**Downgrade needs the same evidence as upgrade.** Do not lower an assessment because one exchange
went badly, and do not raise one because a single exchange went well. A change of one step, with the
evidence that justified it, is the largest honest move a single session supports.

**Never drop what you cannot explain.** If something in the current state looks wrong, retire it
explicitly with a reason. Omitting it is not a correction; it is erosion, and it will be rejected.

**Distinguish observed from inferred.** "Said they work in software" is an observation. "Is a senior
engineer" is an inference. Mark inferences as inferences or leave them out.

**Contradictions are events, not edits.** When new information conflicts with what is stored, record
the correction and the reason. The prior entry is superseded, never deleted.

**No secrets, credentials, addresses, or identifiers** enter the store, whatever the person volunteers.

**Silence is a valid consolidation.** A session that taught you nothing durable produces no entries.
An empty consolidation is correct far more often than an invented one.
